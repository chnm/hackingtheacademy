## Organized Reading (for graduate school and beyond)

By Jana
			
				
As I started graduate school and saw just how much reading I would be doing, I realized that I would need a solid plan to keep all of my notes organized.  I bought a copy of EndNote bibliographic software.  Each book (or article) I read or discussed in a seminar, I added to my EndNote library with the relevant information and notes.
{{< missing-image alt=`3956865159_bd190dc540` src=`http://historycompass.files.wordpress.com/2010/03/3956865159_bd190dc540.jpg?w=300&h=199` >}}


Because my spouse was also a graduate student and our disciplines occasionally overlapped, we  adopted a strategy for organizing our books to avoid duplication and to enable easy retrieval (at that point we had already acquired over two thousand books between us).  We started using BookCollector software to record book data and added a sticker to the spine of each one with the appropriate locational details.  Nonfiction books were shelved by the Library of Congress number.  Fiction books were shelved by category and author’s name.

Recently, I’ve migrated to using [Zotero](http://www.zotero.org/) for book notes (and bibliographies) and we now use [LibraryThing](http://librarything.com/) for book cataloging.  But the basic system is the same: every book we own gets a sticker and an entry in LibraryThing.  Every book I read (or want to read), along with my notes, goes into my Zotero library.

While we’ve been teased a bit by our friends because of our “library” approach to book organization, I’m so glad we made the initial investment in time and organization.  We’ve surely saved quite a bit of money by having an easy way to find all of our books.  Having all of my readings notes in EndNote made prepping for my Comps fairly easy–I was able to review all of my seminar notes and fill in historiographical gaps as needed.  Using Zotero for my prospectus/dissertation has worked wonderfully.

A few months ago my spouse decided to re-organize many of our books by spine color rather than by numerical order.  We only had to alter one field in the LibraryThing catalog to reflect the new shelf plan (adding a tag for color).  Now our books serve as art, as well as being easily retrievable when we need them.

*Want to see [a closer view of our rainbow book project](http://www.flickr.com/photos/mindonfire/3956865159/sizes/l/)?  A few years ago [I recorded a podcast about my organization project](http://makinghistorypodcast.com/2007/11/12/episode-1-using-technology/)–it’s a bit dated now, but still worth a listen if you’re interested in using a similar system.*
