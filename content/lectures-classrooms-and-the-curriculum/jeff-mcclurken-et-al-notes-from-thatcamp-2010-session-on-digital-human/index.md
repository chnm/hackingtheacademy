<b>DH 101 course/DH curriculum</b><br/><br/><b>People in this Session:</b><br/>Tanya Clement; Ethan Watrall; Brian Croxall; Jeff McClurken; Rob Nelson; Scott Nesbit; John M. Jones; Randall Baier; Christina Jenkins; Steve Ramsey; Matthew Slaats; Marc Parry; Steve Kolowich; Jen Serventi; Chad Black; Anastasia Salter; Mark Sample; Clarissa Ceglio<br/>[If you were there and I've missed you, please add yourself.]<br/><br/><b>Issues:</b><br/>Spreading DH approaches to broader undergraduate population <br/><br/>Infrastructure involved in supporting Digital Humanities teaching/programs<br/><br/>"Digitally-inflected humanities" might be a useful way to get undergrads infected with the DH virus<br/><br/><b>Key terms which are almost, but not quite interchangeable in this discussion:</b><br/><table><tr><td>Digital fluency </td><td>Digital literacy<br/></td></tr><tr><td>Information fluency <br/></td><td>Information literacy<br/></td></tr></table><br/><b>Existing programs in DH:</b><br/><a href="http://www.palms.wordherders.net/wp/2009/11/digital-humanities-inflected-undergraduate-programs-2/">http://www.palms.wordherders.net/wp/2009/11/digital-humanities-inflected-undergraduate-programs-2/</a><br/><br/><b>What skills should (undergraduate) students have?:</b><br/><ul><li>scripting</li><li>coding</li><ul><li>kicking them into the deep end of the pool can produce collaboration among students as they have to work to help one another</li></ul><li>databases</li><li>collaboration with those in different fields/specialties</li><ul><li>don't ever have a student do anything on his/her own.</li></ul><li>background knowledge: considerations about AI, privacy, where to learn more when you need to, etc., etc.</li><li>programming</li><li>relational database design</li><li>XML and its descendants </li><li>need to get students to cross over into building. They just need to MAKE something. Work to make the class project-oriented.</li><li>assessment (self-assessment, assessment of other projects, collaborative assessment)</li><li>user experience design/user-centered design</li><li>project management (method or theory)</li><li>game design / playful interaction / models of play (and models for collaboration)</li><li>sustainability / long-term availability of projects / accessible to many groups and geographies </li><li>accessibility</li><li>Writing / creating to face outward</li><ul><li>PR for their own projects </li><li>the audience illusion: generating content for the "wider digital world" doesn't imply a readership</li></ul><li>Discerning the difference between information and knowledge</li><ul><li>Connecting this discernment to producing knowledge</li><li>Helping students recognize where digital information comes from, and how to become producers of knowledge with that information.</li></ul></ul><br/><br/><b>How do you teach them to find a DH-worthy problem?  </b>-- We don't just want to be doing the same analyses (for lack of a better word) that we've been doing for the last X years. We can approach new problems/approaches.<br/><ul><li>having a bit of training behind you will help you to start to see the problems that are worth examining.</li><li>Translation: teach students how to conceptualize traditional problems/approaches to their field in digital environments or with digital tools</li><li>Affordances: teach students how to examine a particular tool (XML, Processing, etc.) and use the affordances of that tool to address a problem, old or new, in their field</li><br/></ul><b>DH Project/Curriculum ideas</b><br/><ul><li>create partnerships where students are looking at each others' work across institutions<br/></li><li>create projects that are oriented around the university itself or its resources, get the library involved as a location for hosting</li><li>get involved with local cultural heritage organizations or NGOs to see if a joint project could be developed</li><li>develop recommendations for how we deal with students' privacy (FERPA) re: posting projects online</li><li>how do we make humanities viral (relevant)?</li><li>create projects that encourage real collaboration, and result in something that is <i>made</i>, (semi-)permanent</li></ul><br/><b>References:</b><br/><ul><li>The Zotero Digital Humanities Group: <a href="http://www.zotero.org/groups/digital_humanities/items">http://www.zotero.org/groups/digital_humanities/items</a></li><li>Syllabi for DH classes on Zotero group -- share your own too.<br/></li></ul><ul><li>http://dh2010.umwblogs.org <br/></li><li>Steve Ramsey's syllabi</li><li>K-12 state technology literacy definitions and standards -- http://www.setda.org/c/document_library/get_file?folderId=247&amp;name=Tech+Lit+MA+11+1+07.doc </li><li>Video of the session, taken by John M.
