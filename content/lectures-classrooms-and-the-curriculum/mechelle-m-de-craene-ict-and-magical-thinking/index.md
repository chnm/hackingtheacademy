Children have very different schemas than adults when relating with technology. The concept of Cybernetic Developmental Theory (De Craene & Cuthell, 2006) uses Piaget's Cognitive Developmental Theory to identify developmental differences in how children interact with technology. This provides a lens through which teachers can understand how children of all ages (including "clickerati") relate to ICT (De Craene & Cuthell, 2006).

This consistent framework can be helpful for teachers because the term "digital literacy" is quite ethereal, ever-changing with technology upgrades. Moreover, "literacy" in itself has become as ubiquitous as the term "postmodernism", leaving it to the subjective interpretation of teachers from one year to the next.

One of the most interesting facets of exploring how chidren related with ICT is magical thinking found in pre-operational children.

Here's a excerpt from one of our research logs:

Exploring Icons (i.e. symbols) and Schemas   The Trash Can Conversation shows elements of magical thinking:

Setting: Gathered around a computer in the classroom (age 6).

Conversation:

T=Teacher (Mechelle)

J=Jenni

B=Breshawn

(\* Children's names have been changed)

T: What do you think some of these little pictures mean?

J: I don't know.

B: Hey, there's little a trashcan.

T: There is? (said with questioning inflection to prompt hoping she'll point)

B: Yes, see it's there. (pointing)

T: Yes, very good!

B: (smiles)

T: Can you put stuff in the trashcan on the computer?

J: (shrugs her shoulders) I don't know.

B: Yes, yes you can…if you have the mouse you can walk around with it and throw it out.

T: hmmm...Can you get something from the trash can once you've thrown it out?

J: No, because it's nasty.

B: You have to get it with the mouse.

J: (speaks at sametime overlapping Breshawn's comment)  But, you can use a glove to get it out.

B: You can't put your hand in the computer.

J: I know.

T: How do you think you empty the trash?

J: Uh, uh, I don't know.

B: Um, um by, hmmm. Clicking on it.

T: Where does it go when you empty the trashcan?

B: It comes right out of the printer.

T: really!? (surprised at her cleaver response)

B: yep. (said confidently)

Conversation continues….

Notes:

Collective Monologue (some overlapping).

Breshawn is equating the virtual trash as coming out of the printer. I thought that was cleaver.
